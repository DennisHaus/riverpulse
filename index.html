DETAILED DESCRIPTION

VIEWER FUNCTIONS The viewer displays either a procedurally generated alpine terrain or an imported PLY, STL, or OBJ model. The camera can be rotated, zoomed, and panned with OrbitControls. The source area can be moved with SHIFT + LEFT CLICK. A custom polygonal source area can be drawn with CMD or CTRL + LEFT CLICK.

TERRAIN REPRESENTATION The terrain is converted into a regular heightfield. Every horizontal position has one terrain height and one interpolated terrain normal. This makes collision calculations efficient. It also means that overhangs, caves, vertical walls, and multiple terrain layers are not represented by the physics.

MACRO-PARTICLES The material volume is divided into macro-particles using: source volume × particles per m³ Each particle represents a volume parcel rather than one physical grain. The equivalent particle radius is calculated from the parcel volume using a spherical-volume approximation. With the default settings, 7,000 m³ and approximately 1,000 particles result in approximately 7 m³ per particle.

GRAVITY AND TERRAIN SLOPE When a particle is in contact with the terrain, gravity is projected onto the tangent plane of the terrain. This produces an explicit downhill acceleration. The normal component of gravity is handled by the terrain contact constraint.

STATIC AND KINETIC FRICTION Static friction prevents particles from creeping on slopes where the downhill driving force is below the static threshold. Once motion begins, kinetic terrain friction reduces the tangential velocity. This creates a clearer distinction between resting material and sliding material.

FLOW DRAG A velocity-dependent quadratic drag is applied to particles moving along the terrain. The drag acceleration increases with the square of the tangential velocity. This limits unrealistically high velocities without requiring a full fluid solver.

PARTICLE CONTACT Nearby particles are found with a spatial hash. Overlapping particles are pushed apart. Relative normal velocity is corrected, tangential contact motion is reduced by material friction, and contact damping reduces jitter and noisy sliding.

COHESION Particle cohesion attracts nearby particles only when they are farther apart than a preferred rest distance. Particles are not artificially attracted when they are already in contact. This reduces excessive clumping while preserving a coherent flow.

COLOUR MODES VELOCITY colours particles according to their current speed. DISTANCE TRAVELED colours them according to accumulated travel distance. FLOW THICKNESS estimates local volume per terrain cell and colours particles according to that local thickness.

SIMULATION CACHE Periodic copies of particle positions, velocities, and traveled distances are stored in memory. The timeline can be used to review cached frames. It does not recompute the simulation backward; it restores previously recorded states.

WHAT THIS IS NOT This is not an MPM solver, FLIP solver, PIC solver, SPH solver, or complete DEM solver. It does not solve pressure, fluid incompressibility, stress tensors, pore-water pressure, erosion, deposition, angular particle rotation, or multiple material densities.

RELIABILITY The simulation is intended for qualitative visualization, design exploration, and comparison of parameter settings. It should not be used for engineering design, hazard prediction, structural loading, legal analysis, or quantitative forecasting of a real debris-flow event.
